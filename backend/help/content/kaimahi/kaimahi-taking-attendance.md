## Opening attendance mode

Open **Sessions**, select the session, then select **Attendance mode**. Attendance mode is designed for walking the room with a tablet. It shows a card for each kaumātua on the roster, with their photo (if photo consent is recorded) or their initial, and a counter of how many are present.

## Marking people present or absent

Tap a card to mark that person present ("✓ Present"). Tap it again to undo a mis-tap. Everyone starts as absent.

While marking attendance, the cards have no absence-reason row beneath them. Marking someone present clears their saved absence reason. Add or correct reasons afterwards through **Review attendance**.

## Finishing: the Done button

When the roll call is complete, select **Done**. This finalises the register:

- if nobody is marked present, you are asked to confirm that this is an empty session

Only finalised registers count in attendance totals and attendance reports, so always select **Done** at the end of a session.

## Correcting a finalised register

Open the finalised session and select **Review attendance**. The present/absent cards are locked, but absent participants have a reason row: **No show**, **Medical appointment**, **Transport issue** or **Other**. **Other** requires a description. **Save reason** saves just that participant’s reason and keeps the review open. **Done** saves all pending reason edits and returns to the session details. The register stays finalised while reasons are reviewed.

To change who was present, select **Reopen attendance**. Pending reason edits are saved before reopening. The reason rows disappear, leaving the same simple participant cards used for roll call. Make your changes and select **Done** to finalise again. If saving fails, the error stays visible and you can retry.

Kaimahi and branch managers can mark, review, finalise and reopen attendance for their own kapa. Super admins can perform the same attendance actions across all kapa, including sessions created by other staff. The audit log identifies who made the change and the session’s kapa.
